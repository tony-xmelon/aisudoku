AI Sudoku - first build on a real device.

Point the camera at a sudoku puzzle. It guides you into position, takes the photo
itself when the framing is good, reads the printed and handwritten digits, and then
lets you ask for a hint, check your own answers, or see the whole solution.

Worth trying, roughly in order of how likely it is to break:

1. Camera guidance. The line at the bottom should change as you move - move closer,
   hold the phone flat, straighten up - and it should fire the shutter on its own once
   it is happy. If it never fires, use the button.
2. An unsolved printed puzzle. This is the case with the most evidence behind it:
   every printed digit was read correctly on all six test photographs.
3. A part-solved puzzle in pencil. Handwriting is the weak point, around 84% per
   digit, so expect the odd misread. Tap a wrong cell to correct it.
4. Check mine, on a puzzle where you know you have made a mistake.
5. Hint. The setting toggles between naming the technique and just giving the digit.

Known and expected:
- Nothing here has ever run on a phone before, so camera behaviour is unverified.
- The grid must be roughly upright and fill a decent part of the frame.
- Small pencilled candidate marks in cell corners are ignored on purpose.
- If it says a photo could not be read, it is refusing rather than guessing - a
  confidently wrong grid is the worst thing it could hand you.
