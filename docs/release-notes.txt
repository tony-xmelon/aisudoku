Seven things this build does that the last one would not.

A PAGE YOU FILLED IN AT THE SIZE OF THE PRINT NOW SCANS

The app finds the printed digits first and everything follows from that: what
is taller is your handwriting, what is smaller is a pencilled candidate. That
rests on the print being the one thing on the page with a single size, and on
your writing being bigger than it - which it is, on every page you have sent.

On a set of newspaper puzzles from another reader it is not. They write at
exactly the size of the print, so the app took their answers for givens and
came out with a puzzle of seventy-odd clues, which is not a puzzle at all. Ten
such pages simply would not read.

They read now. When a page comes back with more printed digits than any sudoku
has, the app sorts that page again by ink instead of by size: print is toner,
laid down heavy and even and standing out sharply from the paper beside it,
where a pen is none of those. It is a finer measure and a riskier one, so it
is used only on a page that has already shown the cheaper test failed on it -
your own pages take exactly the path they took before.

PHOTOGRAPHS TAKEN FURTHER BACK ARE ACCEPTED

The grid had to be 700 pixels across or the app said it was too small to read.
That number was chosen from how many pixels a cell ought to want, and it was
wrong: measured properly, by shrinking every photograph in the collection until
its grid is a given size and reading it again, printed digits come out perfect
down to 360 pixels and handwriting down to 440.

The limit is now 550, which is still well clear of anything that failed. Two
photographs that were refused at 692 and 681 pixels read perfectly once let
through.

A GRID THE APP COULD SEE BUT WOULD NOT TAKE

One photograph came back as no grid found while the puzzle sat squarely in
shot. The outline the app had traced round the grid was a shade inside the
grid's own printed rule - and since it judges a grid on the weakest of the
twenty lines one must have, the two outer rules falling just outside that
outline took the whole score to nothing.

When nothing else has worked, it now tries the best few outlines a couple of
percent larger before giving up. That photograph reads. It costs nothing on a
photograph that was already going to read, because it only runs after
everything else has failed.

A PUZZLE PRINTED HARD AGAINST THE COLUMN BESIDE IT NOW SCANS

Every way the app had of finding a puzzle started by tracing round its border.
On a newspaper page the border often is not there to trace: on one of these
photographs the puzzle's right-hand rule runs into the black bar down the edge
of it and its left-hand one into the horoscope, and what the app finds is one
wide shape that is not a puzzle. It said it could see no grid, on a photograph
you can read at a glance.

It now looks for the squares instead. A border can be lost to whatever is
printed next to it; a square cannot, because it is a hole inside the grid, and
eighty-one holes of the same size packed together is not something else on a
newspaper page. Where the squares are is enough to work out where the puzzle
is, and that page now reads, with all thirty-one of its printed digits right.

It runs only after everything else has failed, so nothing that scanned before
takes a different path.

One photograph from that batch still will not scan, and it is fair to say why:
its page is curved, and enough that no flat shape fits its own squares. That
one needs a flatter photograph rather than a cleverer app.

A CREASED PAGE NOW SCANS

The photograph of the curled page - the one the app said it could see no grid on -
now scans. It was not really the curl that beat it.

The app works out where each square sits in the nine by nine, and when two
squares landed on the same spot one of them was quietly thrown away. On that page
three squares went, all in the same corner - the corner where the paper lifts off
the table - and with nothing there to go on, the app could not work out the shape
of the page just where it bends most. Keeping those three squares is the whole
fix. The page now straightens flat and reads, and all twenty-eight of its printed
digits come out right.

ANSWERS YOU WROTE SMALL ARE NO LONGER THROWN AWAY

The app worked out which digits are yours partly by size, on the assumption that
you write bigger than the paper prints. On the newsprint pages you often do not -
sometimes you write smaller - and every one of those answers was being discarded
as if it were a pencilled note in the margin of the square. Forty-seven of them,
which was more than a third of everything the app was getting wrong.

It now decides by where the digit sits in its square and what it is written in
rather than by how big it is. Twenty-five of those answers come back.

A PUZZLE IT CANNOT SOLVE IS STILL SHOWN TO YOU

If the printed digits did not make a proper puzzle, the app used to give up and
tell you it could not read the photo - even when it had read every digit on the
page correctly. That is two different failures wearing one message, and only one
of them is your problem.

It now stops only when it genuinely cannot see a puzzle. Anything it has read is
shown, with the squares it is unsure of marked, and you can see for yourself what
it made of the page. One of the test photos that used to come back as nothing now
comes back as a grid.

THE READER

Retrained on twenty-four photographs. The collection has grown from six of your
pages to twenty-four, and the handwriting it is measured against from 180 digits
to 753 - newsprint, ballpoint, creases, a curled page, a sudoku on a laptop
screen, and one page finished wrongly, which is the only thing that tests Check
properly.

Scored the honest way, with every page read by a model that has never seen it,
handwriting is 709 of 753 squares and print is 651 of 652. It was 174 of 180 when
the collection was a quarter of the size and all of it was yours; the rate has
held while the collection tripled and got harder.

Please keep sending photos of anything it gets wrong. Every one of these came
from a page that failed.
